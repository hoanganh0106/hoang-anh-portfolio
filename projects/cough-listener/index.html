Cough Listener

A small box for clinic waiting rooms that picks out coughs from background noise and suggests who may need TB or pneumonia screening.

In development

Who it is for

Waiting rooms at district hospitals and commune health stations, where a persistent cough is often the first sign that goes unchecked.

How it works

  1. 1Listen to the room
  2. 2Detect a cough
  3. 3Separate from speech
  4. 4Classify on chip
  5. 5Suggest screening

In detail

The device listens for cough events, separates each cough from speech and room noise using the same source-separation approach as the stethoscope, and classifies the cough’s acoustic pattern on the chip. When a pattern looks concerning it lights up and suggests a referral for screening. Only a short anonymous summary is kept; no conversation is recorded.

My part

  • Designing the cough detection and separation pipeline to fit on a microcontroller.
  • Reusing the three-source separation work from SPMamba and MossFormer 2.
  • Privacy by design: no raw audio or speech is stored.

Hardware design

  • ESP32-S3 microcontroller
  • Two I²S MEMS microphones
  • Status LED ring and small display
  • USB-C power with battery backup

Technology

  • ESP32-S3
  • TinyML
  • Audio event detection
  • Source separation

Status note In development. A screening aid only; it does not diagnose tuberculosis or pneumonia.
