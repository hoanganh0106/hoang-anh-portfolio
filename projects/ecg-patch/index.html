Single-lead ECG Patch

A thin chest patch worn for several days that watches for irregular heart rhythms such as atrial fibrillation and saves only the moments that matter.

In development

Who it is for

Older adults living far from a hospital, for whom a 24-hour Holter test means a long trip and a long wait.

How it works

  1. 1Two-electrode ECG
  2. 2Remove noise
  3. 3Find each beat
  4. 4Rhythm model
  5. 5Save key episodes

In detail

Two electrodes feed a low-power analog front-end. The patch removes muscle and mains noise, finds each heartbeat, and runs a compact model that looks at rhythm irregularity. Instead of streaming everything, it keeps short episodes around suspicious rhythms and hands them to a phone over Bluetooth at the next check-up.

My part

  • Designing the analog front-end and filtering chain for a two-electrode patch.
  • Building a rhythm model small enough for a Bluetooth microcontroller.
  • Event-based storage to stretch battery life over several days.

Hardware design

  • AD8232 ECG front-end
  • nRF52840 Bluetooth Low Energy SoC
  • On-board flash for episodes
  • Thin LiPo cell, flexible PCB

Technology

  • AD8232
  • nRF52840
  • BLE
  • ECG signal processing
  • TinyML

Status note In development. Flags rhythms for a doctor to review; it does not diagnose heart disease.
